Weir GitHub

Know what happened to every item in your pipeline.

Weir counts items as they move through queues, batches and cron jobs. When something goes missing, gets stuck, or takes longer than it should, it tells you which stage, and how many.

What it found

I tested Weir on a small demo pipeline (Kafka, Celery, Postgres and a cron job): I injected faults and compared what Weir reported with the pipeline's own records.

A batch stuck in retries

One bad order made its batch retry forever. The retries were held in the worker's memory, so the queue length stayed at 0. Weir showed 500 orders sitting in the worker, the oldest over 10 minutes old.

Orders that disappeared

A stage can drop messages without raising an error or writing a log line: an early return, a buggy filter, a swallowed exception. Lag and error rates look normal. Weir compares what goes into each stage with what comes out, so it shows which stage lost them and how many.

Waiting on a schedule

Orders started taking three times longer to arrive while every service ran at its usual speed. Weir showed that 412 of the 416 seconds were spent waiting for a cron job.

How it works

An SDK in each service

A few lines per stage. Each item is recorded when it arrives and when it leaves, along with how it left: sent on, finished, dropped, failed, or gone without any of those. A small header carries the item's creation time from stage to stage.

A scraper for what's in between

Items also wait outside your services, in queues and staging tables. The scraper checks those from the outside, so a backlog shows up while it builds, with no code changes.

Low overhead

Only totals leave the process, every few seconds: about 4 µs per message and under 1 KB/s of traffic, whatever the volume. Metric names follow OpenTelemetry's semantic conventions.

import weir

weir.init(pipeline="orders")
enrich = weir.stage("enrich")

with enrich.receive(weir.extract(msg.headers())) as item:
    for line in split(msg):
        producer.produce("lines", line, headers=weir.inject(item.emit()))
